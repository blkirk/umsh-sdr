Decoded packets

RSSI is in-channel power during the packet. SNR is the preamble estimate for weak frames and the power ratio for strong ones. Clipped means the ADC saturated, so RSSI is a lower bound. SRC/DST are the first 4 base58 characters of each key (* where a 3-byte hint can't fix the 4th); "all" is a broadcast, "ch" a multicast channel id, "enc" an address inside an encrypted body, and MAC acks carry none. Hover for the packet type.

Nodes heard

—

One row per radio. Created/Received: packets naming it as SRC/DST, whoever transmitted them; Last created is its newest SRC packet. On air: packets this radio itself transmitted, which is what the SDR measured: its own unrelayed packets plus those it relayed (Relayed, named by the frame's Trace option). On air splits into good, weak (below the noise floor) and clipped (ADC saturated, RSSI a lower bound); median SNR is over On air. A repeater appears under its node key when its 2-byte repeater hint matches exactly one key seen, else as “XX* (repeater)”. Bad-CRC frames and frames relayed by an unnamed hop count only in the totals.
